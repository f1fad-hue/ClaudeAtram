The calm was priced — and it just refused to break

The Hormuz strikes spiked the VIX to 16.34 on 2 September; it fell back to 15.20 the next session and closed Friday at 14.32 — within 0.14 of the 14.18 low it printed on 17 August. The gap between delivered calm and priced risk is wider now than when it looked like it was closing.

Term structure · the math

How these numbers are built

Horizon vol is not read off a single quote — it is integrated out of the forward-variance curve, because variance is what adds across time, not volatility:

σ(0→T) = √( 1⁄T ∫0T σf(t)² dt )

The forward curve σf(t) is bootstrapped piecewise from the three observable points — spot VIX, the September future, the December future — then mean-reverted beyond the last liquid contract toward the long-run VIX anchor of 19.5 at speed κ = 1.5/yr. The realised-equivalent line subtracts the 3.5-point variance risk premium: options have persistently overpriced delivered volatility, and a drawdown model fed on implied vol alone would systematically overstate the damage.

1σ and 2σ are S&P-equivalent moves over the full horizon (σ√T), before any fund's volatility beta is applied.

From index vol to fund vol

Each sleeve inherits the 5-year implied level of —, scaled by its volatility beta, then combined with unhedged peso exposure. The FX leg is not just added noise — the peso tends to weaken when global risk sells off, and for a peso-based holder of USD assets that cushions the loss. Modelled at σFX 6.0%, ρ = −0.20.

σPHP = √( σA² + σFX² + 2ρσAσFX )

How this curve moves the allocation

The term structure is not just description — it is one of the four inputs the optimised portfolio is built from. The signal is the ramp: how far the horizon-blended implied vol sits above spot, using the same 3M 15% / 6M 25% / 12M 30% / 5Y 30% weights as the regional blend. That is how much repricing the option market is still pointing at.

Each sleeve then earns or pays that ramp according to a structural sensitivity — a property of how the fund is built, not a view on the market. Only the covered-call sleeve is paid by a rising ramp.

These tilts are derived from the ramp, not hand-set, and this week shows why that matters: spot fell back toward its 2026 low while the curve held, so the ramp widened from +2.87 to — vol points and every sleeve's tilt moved with it — without anyone re-typing a number.

Turning volatility into a drawdown number

Expected maximum drawdown over a rolling 5-year window is modelled as k × σ − 0.50 × μ — volatility sets the depth, drift claws some of it back. The anchor is k = 1.65, calibrated so the formula reproduces what actually happened at index level: −20% for the S&P 500 (σ 15.5%, μ 11%) and −33% for the Nasdaq-100 (σ 24%, μ 13%), the observed medians for rolling 5-year windows.

1.65 is not the coefficient every sleeve uses. Left tails are not the same shape across these four funds, so each carries a declared adjustment — and the portfolio coefficient is the weighted average across the equity sleeves. Every drawdown printed on this page is reproducible from the k beside it; before 5 September the page showed 1.65 alone, which did not reproduce three of the four.

The money market sleeve is floored separately at a 100bp parallel rate shock on ~0.5y duration, because a cash fund cannot be made to behave like an equity fund by a formula.

Rationale report

Why 15 / 45 / 35 / 5

The recommendation is not "own more of the thing that went up." It is built from one observation: the market is priced for calm it does not expect to keep, and exactly one of these four funds is structurally paid when that changes.

1 · The setup

Three central banks that matter to this portfolio are leaning the same way, and it is the wrong way for long-duration assets. The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% on a 9–3 vote in which the three dissents wanted a hike. The ECB actually delivered one — +25bp to 2.25% in June, its first in three years. The BSP hiked +25bp to 5.00% on 27 August — its third consecutive hike, +75bp cumulative since April. This is no longer a tightening bias; it is a tightening cycle. Be precise about how much of it is priced, though: CME FedWatch puts the 16 September hike at at 58% — one move, not two, and up from 49.4% the day before on the August payrolls beat. A December hike was fully priced a week ago and has since slipped to January 2027. It is happening because Brent at $96 — now up 47% year on year, widened from 40% a week ago, after a Q1 spike to $118 — is pushing headline inflation back up even as core behaves.

Against that, corporate earnings are the strongest pillar in the picture, and it isn't close. Asia ex-Japan consensus has EPS growing roughly 52% this year and 28% next, off the AI and memory cycle, on a forward multiple of 10.5×. That combination — hostile policy, hostile energy, excellent earnings, undemanding valuations — is what produces a 2.65 gauge on the 1–5 scale rather than a 1.9 or a 4.1. It has fallen from 2.9 at the start of September and again this week, and the move this week was a correction, not news: volatility & risk appetite is cut 4.0 to 3.5 on the 1–10 driver scale because last week's claim that the calm was breaking was simply wrong — the VIX gave the whole Hormuz spike back inside three sessions. The week before, inflation was cut from 3.5 to 3.0 — US PCE is running 3.7% over twelve months and 4.1% annualised over six, so the contained 2.5% core CPI this model leaned on is no longer the clean anchor it looked like, and euro inflation jumped to 3.3% in August on energy at +14.3%. Growth went the other way, 5.0 to 5.5, on August payrolls of +162,000 against a 53,000 consensus — which retires the −23k July print this model had been treating as a cracking labour market. It was noise. The two cancel, and the gauge sits where it was.

3 · The two overweights, and why they don't overlap

Asia Equity goes to 35% because it still ranks first on the horizons that matter to a 5-year mandate — — on the 1–5 scale — the near term marked back up this week, because the 2 September selloff this model had read as a persistent energy de-rating reversed inside three sessions on AI and memory demand — and because a 10.5× forward multiple against that earnings growth is the widest valuation-to-growth gap available in these four funds. It is trimmed below the unconstrained maximum for two honest reasons: Korea is already up 71% and Taiwan 49% year to date, so a good deal of the re-rating has been collected; and Asia is the region most exposed to an energy shock it imports, which costs it −0.20pp in the tilt.

Nasdaq Equity Income goes to 45% because it is the best drawdown-adjusted holding on the sheet. Option overwriting cuts its volatility to roughly two-thirds of the raw Nasdaq-100, and unhedged peso exposure trims it again, because the peso tends to weaken precisely when US equities fall. 4 · The cut that pays for it

Global Technology drops from 25% to 5%. This is the least comfortable call on the page, because on pure strategic assumptions it has the highest gross return of the four. Three things override that.

  • It is the same bet, twice. Its correlation with Nasdaq Equity Income is 0.88. Holding both at 25%+ buys almost no diversification — you are paying two fee stacks for one exposure to US mega-cap technology.
  • It is the most rate-sensitive thing you own. Longest-duration equity, in a world where three central banks are leaning hawkish: −0.40pp.
  • It carries a −40% expected drawdown. At a 25% weight that single sleeve contributes more to portfolio drawdown than the money market and Asia sleeves combined.

Cutting it to 5% rather than zero is deliberate. The forecast deliberately sits well below the target fund's realised 15.20% five-year return, and if that AI capex cycle keeps compounding, this is the sleeve that captures it. That option is not free, and the page previously understated it: —

5 · Cash is a position here, not a residual

The money market sleeve is cut from 20% to 15%, which looks backwards for a risk-reducing portfolio. It isn't: the drawdown control is coming from the equity mix, not from hiding in cash, so the cash can be spent on return. What justifies keeping 15% is that peso cash is genuinely paid right now in nominal terms — T-bills at 5.14% to 5.72% after a BSP that has now hiked three times to 5.00%. Be clear about what that is and isn't: against August inflation of 6.1% the real yield still runs from about −0.95pp at the 91-day bill to −0.40pp at the 364-day, so this sleeve is losing purchasing power — considerably more slowly than a month ago, when the same calculation against July's 6.2% gave roughly −1pp across the curve, but losing it. It earns its 15% on zero duration risk and on being the dry powder to rebalance into a drawdown rather than sell into one — not on being a store of real value.

Conclusion

Moving from the baseline to the optimised allocation improves both axes at once.

That is a genuine improvement on both axes at once, not a trade of one for the other — and it comes from re-weighting four funds the investor already holds, with no new products, no leverage and no market timing. The mechanism is simple enough to state in one sentence: swap concentrated long-duration tech beta for the same tech exposure with the volatility sold, and put the proceeds into the cheapest earnings growth available.

What would change this view

  • Brent back below $70 and the Fed cutting. The energy and policy drags reverse; Global Technology goes back to a full weight and the covered-call overweight loses its edge.
  • VIX futures flipping into backwardation. The premium the income sleeve harvests compresses; its — tilt goes to zero — and it is now the largest single tilt in the model, so that reversal would cost more than any other single change on this list.
  • Asia EPS revisions turning negative. The 10.5× multiple is only cheap against the growth; take the growth away and it isn't.

Method & limits

What this model is, and is not

  • Forecasts, not facts. The 5-year CAGR figures are modelled expectations built from published capital-market assumptions and current valuations. They are not guarantees, and no fund's realised return will match them exactly.
  • Two fee estimates. The ongoing charges of the JPMorgan Asia Equity Dividend and Fidelity Global Technology target funds are estimated at 0.80% and 0.95%. Every other fee on this page is a published number.
  • Drawdown is expected, not worst-case. The figures describe a drawdown you should plan to experience. The stress scenarios show the tail.
  • Correlations are stable inputs to an unstable world. The 0.88 between the two US-tech sleeves rises toward 1 in a crisis, which is when diversification is worth most and delivers least.
  • Not personal advice. This is portfolio analysis on four named funds. It does not know your tax position, your income needs, or what else you own.
