Term structure · the math

How these numbers are built

Horizon vol is not read off a single quote — it is integrated out of the forward-variance curve, because variance is what adds across time, not volatility:

σ(0→T) = √( 1⁄T ∫0T σf(t)² dt )

σf(t) is bootstrapped piecewise from — observable points — spot plus — futures, each placed at the centre of the 30-day window it prices — then mean-reverted beyond the last liquid contract toward the long-run anchor of 19.5 at κ = 1.5/yr. The realised-equivalent line subtracts the 3.5-point variance risk premium: options persistently overprice delivered volatility, and a drawdown model fed on implied vol alone would overstate the damage.

1σ and 2σ are S&P-equivalent moves over the full horizon (σ√T), before any fund's volatility beta is applied.

From index vol to fund vol

Each sleeve inherits the 10-year implied level of —, scaled by its volatility beta, then combined with unhedged peso exposure. The FX leg is not just added noise — the peso tends to weaken when global risk sells off, and for a peso-based holder of USD assets that cushions the loss. Modelled at σFX 6.0%, ρ = −0.20.

σPHP = √( σA² + σFX² + 2ρσAσFX )

How this curve moves the allocation

The signal is the gap between the horizon-blended curve and the long-run level every fund's base return already assumes, on the same — weights as the regional blend.

Each sleeve then earns or pays that gap according to a structural sensitivity — a property of how the fund is built, not a view on the market. The covered-call sleeve gains most when the curve sits above long-run, and loses most when it sits below.

These tilts are derived from the gap, not hand-set. Until 25 September the gap was measured from spot, which paid the covered-call sleeve for volatility rising to a level its base return already assumed.

Turning volatility into a drawdown number

Expected maximum drawdown is modelled as (k × σ − 0.50 × μ) × √(T / 5) — volatility sets the depth, drift claws some back, and the horizon decides how much room the path has to find its worst peak-to-trough. The anchor k = 1.65 is calibrated to reproduce the observed medians over rolling five-year windows: −20% for the S&P 500 (σ 15.5%, μ 11%) and −33% for the Nasdaq-100 (σ 24%, μ 13%).

A longer mandate cannot just relabel those anchors. Expected maximum drawdown grows with the square root of the horizon, so the calibrated quantity is scaled by — — derived from the five-year calibration rather than re-fitted by eye to ten-year medians this model has not verified.

1.65 is not the coefficient every sleeve uses. Left tails differ across these four funds, so each carries a declared adjustment, and the portfolio coefficient is the weighted average across the equity sleeves. Every drawdown printed here is reproducible from the k beside it.

The money market sleeve is floored separately at a 100bp parallel rate shock on ~0.5y duration — a cash fund cannot be made to behave like an equity fund by a formula. That floor is deliberately not horizon-scaled.

    Method & limits

    What this model is, and is not

    • Forecasts, not facts. The 10-year CAGR figures are modelled expectations built from published capital-market assumptions and current valuations. They are not guarantees, and no fund's realised return will match them exactly.
    • One fee estimate left. Every fee here is a published number except the JPMorgan Asia Equity Dividend ongoing charge, taken at 1.55% — anchored on J.P. Morgan's published 1.50% management fee. The target is a Hong Kong unit trust, whose trustee and admin costs may put the true figure higher.
    • Drawdown is expected, not worst-case. The figures describe a drawdown you should plan to experience. The stress scenarios show the tail.
    • Correlations are stable inputs to an unstable world. The 0.88 between the two US-tech sleeves rises toward 1 in a crisis, which is when diversification is worth most and delivers least.
    • Not personal advice. This is portfolio analysis on four named funds. It does not know your tax position, your income needs, or what else you own.
